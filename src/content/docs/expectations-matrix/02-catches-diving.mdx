A cross-age reference of skill benchmarks for youth goalkeepers, outlining when each technical phase becomes a developmental priority. The matrix below prioritizes safe joint impact and hand shape mastery before introducing high-velocity diving mechanics.

<Aside type="tip" title="Coaching Philosophy">
  Safe mechanics precede athletic power. Never force a goalkeeper into extension diving before they have mastered ground absorption and fundamental hand shapes.
</Aside>

## Age-Band Developmental Focus

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="6-9 Yrs: Ground Handling Foundation" icon="setting">
    **Core Focus:** Basket catches, ground control, and low body positioning. 

    **Safety First:** Avoid high-impact collapse diving to protect developing growth plates and joints. Adapt to smaller goals (such as 6 × 4 ft or 12 × 6 ft) to ensure appropriate success rates and confidence.
  </Card>
  <Card title="9-12 Yrs: Diving & Reaction Expansion" icon="approve-check">
    **Core Focus:** Contour hand shapes (W-catch) and structured collapse diving.

    **The 9v9 Shift:** As goal sizes expand to 18.5 × 6.5 ft, introduce ground absorption mechanics. Address psychological fear of impact by starting all collapse dives from a kneeling or low-squat position before progressing to standing starts.
  </Card>
  <Card title="12-14 Yrs: Specialization & Power" icon="rocket">
    **Core Focus:** Airborne extension dives and high-velocity parrying control.

    **11v11 Mastery:** Full-size goal integration (24 × 8 ft) matches physical maturity. Account for adolescent growth spurts where temporary loss of coordination or joint stiffness occurs, adjusting training loads accordingly.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

<Aside type="caution" title="Managing Growth Spurts">
  During rapid adolescent growth spurts (ages 12-14), young goalkeepers often experience temporary decreases in dynamic balance and spatial awareness. Temporarily scale back high-impact plyometric diving loads to prevent Osgood-Schlatter or patellar tendinitis.
</Aside>
