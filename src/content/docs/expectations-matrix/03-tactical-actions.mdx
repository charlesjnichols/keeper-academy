Distribution and box control progress from simple underhand rolls and short passes to complex 1v1 block shapes and full-field playmaking. The matrix below outlines when each technical and tactical action becomes a developmental priority.

<Aside type="tip" title="Modern Build-Up Philosophy">
  While punting is historically taught, modern coaching prioritizes short, accurate distribution and driven build-up passes to retain possession under pressure rather than surrendering the ball in the air.
</Aside>

## Age-Band Developmental Focus

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="6-9 Yrs: Short Distribution & Tracking" icon="setting">
    **Core Focus:** Underhand rolls, short inside-foot passes, and basic ground tracking.

    **Building Confidence:** Establish early comfort with using hands and feet close to the goal line. Emphasize simple verbal cues like "keeper's ball" to encourage basic communication with defenders.
  </Card>
  <Card title="9-12 Yrs: Box Control & Transition" icon="approve-check">
    **Core Focus:** Sling throws, structured punts, K-Block shapes, and high ball claims.

    **The 9v9 Shift:** As the field expands, introduce the K-Block (expanding surface area defensively while staying balanced) and contour catching on flighted crosses. Train goalkeepers to recognize when to distribute wide vs. central.
  </Card>
  <Card title="12-14 Yrs: Physical Contact & Playmaking" icon="rocket">
    **Core Focus:** Sliding smothers, aerial punching, and advanced side-volley distribution.

    **11v11 Mastery:** Full-field tactical immersion requires high-velocity driven passes and calculated risk-taking in 1v1 breakaway scenarios. Prioritize timing and body protection during sliding challenges to minimize collision trauma.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

<Aside type="caution" title="Managing 1v1 Collisions">
  When teaching sliding smothers and aggressive 1v1 challenges, prioritize the "cork" or side-body absorption technique. Goalkeepers must lead with their body contour rather than exposed joints to prevent head-on collisions with onrushing attackers.
</Aside>
