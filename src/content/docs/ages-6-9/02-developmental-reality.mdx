---
title: Developmental Reality
description: Physical, ball-familiarity, cognitive, and social foundations at ages 6-9.
---

The youngest goalkeepers learn through play. At ages 6 to 9, the primary objective is ensuring players fall in love with moving their bodies and kicking a ball, not winning Saturday morning scorelines. This module builds the athletic base, ball familiarity, and emotional safety that every future keeper relies on.

## Physical Development

**Multi-Sport Agility:** Repeated play in running, jumping, hopping, throwing, and catching builds the athletic base every goalkeeper will later rely on. A size 3 ball with plenty of touches keeps hands and feet busy.

## Ball Familiarity

**Hands-On the Game:** Frequent touches of the ball build confidence and feel. Rolling, scooping, throwing, and chasing keep the young keeper connected to play rather than stuck between the posts.

## Cognitive Growth

**Egocentric to Aware:** Ages 6-7 chase the ball in a tight "beehive" cluster focused entirely on the ball. Ages 8-9 begin recognizing teammates, opponents, and space, the first building blocks of tactical vision.

## Social Development

**Play Together, Learn Together:** Friendships and shared fun anchor this age. Praise effort and belonging, not just the role, so the keeper stays part of the squad rather than set apart from it.
