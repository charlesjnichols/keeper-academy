The stance and footwork alignment provide the dynamic balance required for explosive movement. Mastering foot position optimizes weight distribution and reaction time for immediate lateral mobility.

<Aside type="tip" title="Coaching Philosophy">
  A goalkeeper's athleticism is only as good as their foundation. If a player is heavy on their heels, even the fastest reactions will be delayed by a split second.
</Aside>

## Mechanical Breakdown

<Steps>
  1. **Base Width:** Set feet shoulder-width to slightly wider, creating a stable, balanced foundation without locking the hip joints.
  2. **Knee Flexion:** Maintain a soft bend in the knees to keep the center of gravity low and ready for immediate lateral push-off.
  3. **Weight Distribution:** Load the metatarsals (balls of the feet) so the heels hover lightly off the turf.
  4. **Set Timing (Split Step):** Execute a micro-bounce timing adjustment just as the shooter strikes the ball to absorb and reload kinetic energy.
  5. **Toe Alignment:** Point toes toward the ball line to ensure instant forward or diagonal movement mechanics.
</Steps>

## Coaching Framework

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Verbal Cues" icon="pencil">
    **Instant Field Triggers:** Use short, rhythmic phrases during practice sessions to instill proper habits. 

    *Light on toes* | *Set on the strike* | *Push off the back foot*
  </Card>
  <Card title="Common Corrections" icon="warning">
    **Targeted Fixes:** Address mechanical breakdowns immediately before they become ingrained habits.

    **Flat-Footed / Heel Heavy:** Correct backward falling and delayed lateral response by forcing heel lift.
    
    **Stance Too Wide:** Fix trapped or locked movement by narrowing the base back to shoulder width.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

<Aside type="caution" title="Managing Young Growth Plates">
  For younger goalkeepers (ages 6-9), avoid excessive repetitive plyometric bouncing during stance training to protect developing ankle and knee joints while they build baseline core strength.
</Aside>
