Redirecting high-velocity or hard-to-hold shots with the palms to a safe area, rather than attempting an unsafe catch or presenting a rebound to onrushing attackers. Parrying is an essential damage-control skill when shot power exceeds safe catching capacity.

<Aside type="tip" title="Coaching Philosophy">
  If you cannot catch it cleanly, push it where nobody can reach it. A controlled deflection out to the touchline or over the crossbar is infinitely better than a soft catch that spills into the center of the box.
</Aside>

## Mechanical Breakdown

<Steps>
  1. **Early Decision:** Judge early in the ball's flight path whether it can be safely caught; commit to parrying when a clean hold is impossible.
  2. **Firm Surface Creation:** Meet the ball with open palms, fingers spread wide, and wrists locked rigid to absorb and redirect the kinetic energy.
  3. **Directional Deflection:** 
     - **High Balls:** Push the ball up and over the crossbar.
     - **Low/Mid Balls:** Redirect the ball wide and forward toward the flanks, aiming for the touchline or corner flags.
  4. **Avoid Danger:** Never parry the ball back into the central corridor or penalty spot where attacking players are crashing.
</Steps>

## Coaching Framework

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Verbal Cues" icon="pencil">
    **Instant Field Triggers:** Use these concise phrases during shooting drills to guide redirection.

    *Make it wide* → *Over the bar* → *Firm wrists, open palms*
  </Card>
  <Card title="Common Corrections" icon="warning">
    **Targeted Fixes:** Correct common parrying breakdowns immediately.

    **Parrying into Center Corridor:** Deflecting balls back into the middle results in easy tap-ins for attackers. Enforce pushing balls wide.
    
    **Soft Wrists on Impact:** Weak wrists cause the ball to slip through the hands or deflect unpredictably. Cue *lock the wrists*.

    **Staying Down After Deflection:** Goalkeepers often watch their parry instead of recovering. Enforce an immediate secondary recovery movement to get back to their feet.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

<Aside type="caution" title="Training Progression">
  Introduce parrying drills using high-velocity service from short distances only after goalkeepers have mastered wrist stability and hand shaping on standard catches. Emphasize that parrying is an active redirection, not a passive block.
</Aside>
