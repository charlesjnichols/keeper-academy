Managing 1v1 duels when an attacker penetrates the defensive line. Closing down space, setting position, and executing block or spread saves requires immense patience and spatial awareness.

<Aside type="tip" title="Coaching Philosophy">
  In a 1v1, time is the goalkeeper's greatest ally. The longer you stay on your feet and big in your frame, the more pressure you place on the attacker to make a mistake.
</Aside>

## Mechanical Breakdown

<Steps>
  1. **Close Distance on Heavy Touches:** Close the distance rapidly only while the attacker's head is down or their touch is heavy. Stop short of lunging.
  2. **Angle Control & Setting:** Bisect the angle between the ball and the center of the goal. Shorten your stride and set balanced as the attacker prepares to strike.
  3. **Barrier Expansion:** Drop into a K-block or star spread to maximize surface coverage, reducing the available goal frame.
  4. **Patience & Timing:** Maintain extreme patience, staying on your feet as long as possible to force the attacker's decision rather than committing prematurely.
</Steps>

## Coaching Framework

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Verbal Cues" icon="pencil">
    **Instant Field Triggers:** Use these concise phrases during 1v1 training drills.

    *Attack heavy touches* → *Set on the strike* → *Stay big, stay patient*
  </Card>
  <Card title="Common Corrections" icon="warning">
    **Targeted Fixes:** Correct common breakdown habits immediately.

    **Lunging Too Early:** Committing to a dive too soon allows the attacker to easily round the goalkeeper. Enforce staying upright and patient.
    
    **Stopping Too Close / Getting Chipped:** Rushing right up to the attacker's feet leaves room for a chip shot. Stop roughly one to two yards away, maintaining a balanced ready frame.

    **Narrow Stance:** Standing tall and narrow makes it easy for low shots to pass through. Expand the base into a block shape.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

<Aside type="caution" title="Managing Recovery Context">
  Teach goalkeepers to scan for recovering defenders before executing a full spread block. If a defender is actively tracking back and applying pressure from behind, the goalkeeper can delay their challenge further, trusting the backline to help shut down passing and shooting lanes.
</Aside>
