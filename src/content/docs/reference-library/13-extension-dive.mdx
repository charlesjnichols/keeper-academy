An explosive lateral push used to reach mid-height and wide shots that lie beyond simple collapse-dive range. This is an advanced athletic maneuver requiring high lower-body power and precise mid-air body control.

<Aside type="tip" title="Coaching Philosophy">
  Extension dives are high-impact power skills. Never introduce full airborne extension diving until the goalkeeper has completely mastered ground absorption and collapse dives from a standing position.
</Aside>

## Mechanical Breakdown

<Steps>
  1. **The Power Step:** Step diagonally toward the ball trajectory with the near foot, loading body weight into the pushing leg.
  2. **Explosive Leg Drive:** Drive forcefully off the near leg, extending the hip, knee, and ankle upward and outward toward the ball's flight path.
  3. **Flight & Hand Extension:** Extend both hands together along the ball's line of flight, locking the contour shape before impact.
  4. **Sequential Absorption:** Land sequentially across the outer leg, hip, and side of the torso. Never land flat on the stomach or directly on elbows and knees.
</Steps>

## Coaching Framework

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Verbal Cues" icon="pencil">
    **Instant Field Triggers:** Use these phrases during advanced handling sessions to maintain form.

    *Power step* → *Drive the hip* → *Land on your side*
  </Card>
  <Card title="Common Corrections" icon="warning">
    **Targeted Fixes:** Correct common airborne breakdowns immediately to protect player joints.

    **Jumping Upward Instead of Outward:** Jumping straight up limits lateral reach. Enforce pushing through the line of the ball.
    
    **Landing Flat on Stomach:** Slapping the ground flat causes breath-taking impacts. Ensure a rolling side-landing across the hip and thigh.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

<Aside type="caution" title="Managing Physical Maturity & Fear">
  Extension diving introduces a psychological barrier regarding impact force and height. Build confidence progressively by starting with short-distance, low-height extension drops onto crash mats or soft turf before moving to full-speed, top-corner shot stopping.
</Aside>
