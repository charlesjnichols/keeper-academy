A powerful medium-to-long distance distribution method used to bypass pressing attackers and instantly spark counter-attacks. The overhand throw combines accuracy with high velocity, allowing goalkeepers to target players breaking down the flanks.

<Aside type="tip" title="Coaching Philosophy">
  An overhand throw is often faster and more accurate than a driven punt. Use it when you spot an open winger making a transition run before the opposing defense can organize.
</Aside>

## Mechanical Breakdown

<Steps>
  1. **Grip & Stance:** Cup the ball firmly in your dominant hand with fingers spread wide; turn your non-throwing shoulder toward your intended target.
  2. **Wind-Up:** Wind the ball back behind your head, flexing the elbow to roughly a 90° angle while shifting weight onto your back foot.
  3. **Kinetic Chain & Step:** Step forcefully forward onto your lead foot, uncoiling your hips and torso to generate rotational power from the ground up.
  4. **Whip & Release:** Whip your throwing arm over your shoulder with a sharp wrist snap, driving the ball on a flat, low-trajectory line to your teammate.
</Steps>

## Coaching Framework

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Verbal Cues" icon="pencil">
    **Instant Field Triggers:** Use these concise phrases during transition drills.

    *Point, step, whip* → *Driven, not lofted* → *Rotate hips*
  </Card>
  <Card title="Common Corrections" icon="warning">
    **Targeted Fixes:** Correct common throwing breakdowns immediately.

    **Lofting the Ball:** Throwing upward like a basketball arc gives defenders time to intercept. Enforce a flat, driven trajectory.
    
    **Lack of Body Rotation:** Throwing solely with arm strength limits distance and stresses the shoulder. Cue *rotate the hips*.

    **Stepping with Wrong Foot:** Stepping with the same-side foot blocks power generation. Ensure a clean step with the opposite foot.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

<Aside type="caution" title="Shoulder Health & Age Appropriateness">
  Overhand throwing places high rotational stress on the shoulder joint. Do not force repetitive long-distance overhand throws on younger goalkeepers (under 12) whose joints are still developing. Prioritize rolling and sling distributions until physical maturity catches up.
</Aside>
