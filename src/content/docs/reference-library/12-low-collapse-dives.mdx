A controlled lateral drop save used to reach low ground shots without leaving the turf in an airborne jump. Mastering the collapse dive is essential for efficient shot-stopping while protecting developing joints from hard impacts.

<Aside type="tip" title="Coaching Philosophy">
  A collapse dive is a controlled descent, not a fall. Teach goalkeepers to "crumble" downward from the ground up rather than jumping sideways into the air.
</Aside>

## Mechanical Breakdown

<Steps>
  1. **Step & Set:** Step diagonally toward the ball line with the near foot to establish direction and momentum.
  2. **Collapse:** Collapse the near knee and hip downward to drop the lower body quickly to turf level.
  3. **Lead with Hands:** Lead with both hands behind the trajectory of the ball, securing it with a firm contour shape.
  4. **Absorption Landing:** Land sequentially on the outer thigh, hip, and upper torso. Never land directly on elbows or knees.
</Steps>

## Coaching Framework

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Verbal Cues" icon="pencil">
    **Instant Field Triggers:** Use these phrases during diving sessions to reinforce rhythm.

    *Step, collapse, slide* → *Hands lead the way* → *Crumble, don't jump*
  </Card>
  <Card title="Common Corrections" icon="warning">
    **Targeted Fixes:** Correct fundamental breakdown habits immediately.

    **Jumping Sideways:** Launching upward off both feet creates hard, unsafe landings. Enforce dropping downward.
    
    **Landing on Elbows/Knees:** Protect joints by ensuring the outer thigh and hip absorb the primary contact force.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

<Aside type="caution" title="Overcoming Fear of Impact">
  For goalkeepers new to diving, remove the psychological barrier by starting all collapse training from a kneeling or low-squat position. Only progress to standing starts once they demonstrate smooth, pain-free ground absorption on soft grass or turf mats.
</Aside>
