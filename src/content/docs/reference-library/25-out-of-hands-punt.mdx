A high-clearing kicking restart out of hands designed to achieve maximum distance upfield into the opponent's half. While historically the primary method of distribution, modern coaching frameworks treat the punt as a situational tool rather than a default strategy.

<Aside type="tip" title="Coaching Philosophy">
  Prioritize short build-up play and throwing before resorting to a punt. Teach goalkeepers to punt only when under severe immediate pressure or when breaking a high opponent press late in a match.
</Aside>

## Mechanical Breakdown

<Steps>
  1. **Grip & Stance:** Hold the ball waist-high with your guiding hand supporting the side; plant your non-kicking foot firmly on the turf.
  2. **The Drop:** Drop the ball straight down toward your kicking foot. Never toss the ball upward, as a high toss ruins trajectory timing.
  3. **The Strike:** Swing your kicking leg with a rigidly locked ankle, striking the center of the ball on your laces just below waist height.
  4. **Follow-Through:** Follow through smoothly in the direction of the target, landing forward safely on your kicking foot.
</Steps>

## Coaching Framework

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Verbal Cues" icon="pencil">
    **Instant Field Triggers:** Use these concise phrases during distribution sessions.

    *Drop, don't toss* → *Lock the ankle* → *Laces through ball center*
  </Card>
  <Card title="Common Corrections" icon="warning">
    **Targeted Fixes:** Correct fundamental punt breakdowns immediately.

    **Tossing the Ball Up:** Tossing the ball into the air causes it to drop unpredictably, leading to mistimed kicks and shins. Enforce dropping it straight down.
    
    **Loose Ankle / Floppy Foot:** Hitting the ball with a loose ankle hurts the foot and sends the ball spinning wildly off target. Cue: *Make your foot a solid block.*

    **Leaning Backward:** Leaning back during the swing sends the ball straight up into the air instead of forward. Enforce a forward chest posture.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

<Aside type="caution" title="Youth Joint Safety & Tactical Rules">
  Repetitive punting places extreme rotational stress on developing lower backs, hips, and knees. Limit heavy punting drills in youth training (ages 6 to 12). Furthermore, keep in mind that many modern small-sided youth leagues restrict or ban punting entirely to encourage ground-level build-up play.
</Aside>
